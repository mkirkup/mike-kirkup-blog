Below-market salary for real shares is an ownership bet. Below-market salary for options usually means giving up cash for the right to pay more cash later.

If you asked me whether you should take stock options in place of salary, my answer would be blunt: under no circumstances should you defer salary for stock options.

You can take below-market cash for actual shares in a company you believe in. That is a real ownership trade, with real risk. What you should not do is give up cash you could take home for the right to pay more cash later.

The trap is "$X in options." It sounds like $X of stock. It usually is not. Share count times strike price often produces a round number that looks like compensation. That number is exercise cost—the cheque you would still write to buy the shares after you already gave up salary.

Run the same $40,000 trade two ways. In both, you forgo $40,000 of cash. In one, you receive 20,000 actual shares at an initial price of $2.00. In the other, you receive 20,000 options with a $2.00 strike—again labelled "$40,000 in options," because 20,000 × $2.00 = $40,000.

Now put the share price at $1.50. The shares are worth $30,000 on paper. You are down $10,000 from the starting mark, and private stock may not be liquid, but you still own something worth $30,000. The options have no intrinsic value at $1.50 against a $2.00 strike. They may retain some time value, but to own the same 20,000 shares you would still need to write a $40,000 exercise cheque—after already forgoing $40,000 of salary.

That is the contrast that matters. Same cash given up. Same share count. Same drop in price. One path leaves you holding ownership. The other leaves you holding a right that is underwater, and still expensive to exercise.

If options were a good thing to exchange for cash, equity investors would routinely put money in for options instead of ownership. They generally do not. They exchange cash for shares—or for instruments that convert into ownership—because what they want is ownership, not merely the right to pay again later.

Take below-market salary for ownership if that is the bet you want to make. Do not defer salary for options and tell yourself you were paid.
